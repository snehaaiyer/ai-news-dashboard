Core Concept

What is Technical Analysis?

Technical Analysis (TA) is a research technique that identifies trading opportunities based on the actions of market participants, visualized through stock charts. Over time, patterns form within these charts and each pattern conveys a specific message about likely future price movement.

Key Insight: Unlike Fundamental Analysis which studies a company's financials, TA only cares about historical price and volume data. The same TA concepts work across equities, commodities, forex, and crypto.
Four Assumptions

The Pillars of Technical Analysis

TA rests on four foundational assumptions that every practitioner must internalize:

1. Markets Discount Everything — All known and unknown information (earnings, insider activity, macro events) is already reflected in the current price. You don't need to know WHY something happened; the price chart shows you that it DID.
2. The "How" > The "Why" — A technical analyst doesn't question why an insider bought stock. They only observe that the price reacted, confirming buying interest.
3. Price Moves in Trends — Markets don't move randomly. NIFTY going from 6400 to 7700 happened in a phased, directional manner over 11 months — not overnight. Once a trend establishes, price tends to follow it.
4. History Tends to Repeat Itself — Market participants react consistently to similar situations. Greed in uptrends and fear in downtrends create repeating chart patterns.

OHLC

The Trade Summary — Open, High, Low, Close

Every trading day produces millions of individual trades. Instead of tracking each one, we summarize with four key data points:

Open: The first trade price when markets open (9:15 AM IST)
High: The highest price reached during the session
Low: The lowest price reached during the session
Close: The final trade price at market close (3:30 PM IST) — this is the MOST important as it reflects the final consensus of the day

Chart Types

Three Chart Types in Technical Analysis

Each trading day produces 4 data points (OHLC). Regular charts like pie or column charts can't display all 4 simultaneously. TA uses three specialized chart types:

ChartData PointsProsCons
Line ChartClose onlySimple, shows trend at a glanceIgnores Open, High, Low
Bar ChartAll 4 (OHLC)Complete dataHard to read, lacks visual appeal
CandlestickAll 4 (OHLC)Visual, pattern-friendly, industry standardRequires learning patterns
Verdict: Japanese Candlesticks are the gold standard. They originated in 18th century Japan (rice merchant Homma Munehisa) and were introduced to the West by Steve Nison in the 1980s.

Understanding the Candlestick Structure

Real Body = Rectangle connecting Open and Close. Upper Shadow = Line from body top to High. Lower Shadow = Line from body bottom to Low. Long body = strong conviction. Short body = subdued activity.

Three Golden Rules

Candlestick Trading Rules

Rule 1: Buy Strength, Sell Weakness — Buy on bullish (blue) candle days. Sell/short on bearish (red) candle days. Strength = blue candle, Weakness = red candle.
Rule 2: Be Flexible, Quantify & Verify — Real-world candles rarely match textbook definitions exactly. Allow minor variations but always quantify them (e.g., if High vs Close difference is 0.17%, that's acceptable).
Rule 3: Look for Prior Trend — Bullish patterns need a prior downtrend. Bearish patterns need a prior uptrend. Exception: Marubozu works regardless of prior trend.
Pattern: Marubozu

The Marubozu ("Bald" in Japanese)

A candle with NO upper or lower shadows — just the real body. It's the strongest single candle signal.

Bullish Marubozu
Open=Low, Close=High

Bearish Marubozu
Open=High, Close=Low

Bullish Marubozu: Buy around closing price. Stoploss = Low of the candle. Signals extreme buying interest.
Bearish Marubozu: Short around closing price. Stoploss = High of the candle. Signals extreme selling pressure.
Pattern: Spinning Top & Doji

Indecision Candles

Spinning Top
Small body, long shadows

Doji
Open = Close (no body)

Spinning Top: Bulls tried going up (upper shadow), bears tried going down (lower shadow), neither won → indecision. Color doesn't matter.
Doji: Even more extreme indecision (Open = Close exactly). Think of them as "the calm before the storm."

In a downtrend: could signal reversal (bears losing power) or continuation (bears consolidating). In an uptrend: could signal reversal (bulls fatigued) or continuation (bulls pausing).

Strategy: When you see spinning tops/dojis, reduce position size to 50%. If market reverses, you profit on half. If it continues, you're still invested with half.
Pattern: Paper Umbrella

Hammer & Hanging Man

Both look identical — small body at top, long lower shadow (at least 2x the body length), little/no upper shadow. The NAME and MEANING depends on where they appear:

Hammer
(at bottom of downtrend)
BULLISH

Hanging Man
(at top of uptrend)
BEARISH

Hammer trade: Go long. Stoploss = Low of hammer. Prior trend MUST be down.
Hanging Man trade: Go short. Stoploss = High of hanging man. Prior trend MUST be up.
Pattern: Shooting Star

The Inverted Paper Umbrella

Shooting Star
(at top of uptrend)
BEARISH

Long upper shadow (at least 2x body), small body at bottom, little/no lower shadow. Shows bulls pushed price high but bears dragged it back down.

Trade: Short around close. Stoploss = High of the candle. Prior trend MUST be bullish. More reliable with a red body.
The Trade Trap

Avoid Candles That Are Too Short or Too Long

Too Short (range < 1%): Subdued trading; hard to identify direction. Signal is weak.
Too Long (range > 10%): Stoploss will be very deep. If the trade goes wrong, the loss is painful.

Stick to candles with moderate range for the best risk-reward setups.

Multi-Candle Patterns

The Engulfing Pattern

Formed by two candles where the second candle's body completely "engulfs" (covers) the first candle's body.

Day 1: small red
Day 2: large blue engulfs
BULLISH

Day 1: small blue
Day 2: large red engulfs
BEARISH

Bullish Engulfing: Appears in downtrend. Day 1 = small red. Day 2 = large blue that opens below Day 1's close and closes above Day 1's open. Buy at Day 2's close, Stoploss = Low of the pattern.
Bearish Engulfing: Appears in uptrend. Day 1 = small blue. Day 2 = large red that opens above Day 1's close and closes below Day 1's open. Short at Day 2's close, Stoploss = High of the pattern.
Piercing Pattern & Dark Cloud Cover

Partial Engulfing Cousins

Piercing Pattern (Bullish): In a downtrend. Day 1 = long red candle. Day 2 = blue candle that opens below Day 1's low but closes at least 50% INTO Day 1's body (not completely engulfing). Think: "piercing through the bearish sentiment."
Dark Cloud Cover (Bearish): In an uptrend. Day 1 = long blue candle. Day 2 = red candle that opens above Day 1's high but closes at least 50% INTO Day 1's body. Think: "dark clouds covering the bullish sky."

Both are weaker signals than full engulfing patterns because the reversal isn't as convincing — the second candle only covers half, not all, of the first.

The Harami Pattern

The Opposite of Engulfing

"Harami" means "pregnant" in Japanese. The second candle is SMALL and fits INSIDE the first candle's body.

Day 1: large red
Day 2: small blue inside
BULLISH

Day 1: large blue
Day 2: small red inside
BEARISH

Bullish Harami: In downtrend. Day 1 = big red. Day 2 = small blue that fits within Day 1's range. The small Day 2 shows sellers are losing steam. Buy at Day 2 close, Stoploss = Low of Day 1.
Bearish Harami: In uptrend. Day 1 = big blue. Day 2 = small red inside. Buyers losing steam. Short at Day 2 close, Stoploss = High of Day 1.
Doji in Multi-Candle Context

The Presence of a Doji (Special Engulfing)

When a Doji appears as the second candle in an engulfing pattern, it adds an extra layer of indecision. A Doji after a long red candle in a downtrend is a particularly strong bullish signal — the selling completely exhausted and the market is at equilibrium.

Trade with more conviction when a Doji forms after a strong trend candle. The contrast between conviction (Day 1) and indecision (Day 2) is a powerful reversal signal.
